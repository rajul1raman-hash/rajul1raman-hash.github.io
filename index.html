Case 01Clinic operationsSynthetic data · 12 weeks · 4 therapists

Full diaries, stalled revenue.

01 Presenting problemWhat the owner could see

A four-therapist physiotherapy clinic looked busy every week, yet revenue would not move and nobody could say why.

Appointments, no-shows, therapist hours and invoices lived in separate places, so the owner had volume but no view of what a booked slot was actually worth. I defined four numbers to answer with: booked appointments, no-show rate, share of therapist capacity used, and revenue.

02 DiagnosisSlice the diary until the leak shows
Therapist
Visit type
Booked ahead

    Cut by visit type and booking lead time, the pattern isolates itself: first visits booked more than five days ahead no-show at 27.6% in weeks 1–6, against 4.9–11.4% for every other segment. Try the filters: pick “First visit” and “>5 days”.

    03 TreatmentThe smallest change that hits the leak

    From week 7 (the dashed line on the chart): an automated reminder for any visit booked more than five days ahead, and up to two overbooked slots per therapist per week on far-ahead first visits, so a no-show no longer leaves an empty hour. In the model, nothing else changes: same therapists, same fees, same hours.

    Synthetic outcome, effect built into the generator

    Synthetic data: the treatment effect was written into the generator, so this shows how I would measure a change like this, not that it would happen. Generator and seed are in the repository (tools/gen_data.py).

    Case 02E-pharmacy funnelSynthetic numbers · 10,000 visits

    Traffic climbing, orders standing still.

    01 Presenting problemWhat the dashboard said

    An online pharmacy’s visits kept rising while orders did not. Where do people leave, and do first-time buyers come back?

    Structured on the kind of funnel and conversion tracking I set up at imcure (Google Ads conversion tracking, partner-API journeys). No real company, customer or order data is shown.

    02 DiagnosisEvent funnel and monthly cohorts
    Funnel scenario

    The biggest leak is not traffic. Browsing drop-off is expected. Among shoppers with intent, 1,180 of 1,900 carts (62%) never reach checkout: the steepest fall after the product page, and the step where shipping cost and prescription upload first appear.

    Repeat purchase by first-order month (synthetic)

    Retention is improving cohort over cohort (month-1 return 24% → 27% → 31%), so the problem is conversion earlier in the funnel, not loyalty after it.

    03 TreatmentRanked by orders recoverable

    Three changes, in order: guest checkout (no forced sign-up before payment); prescription upload at the cart, with a clear reason if a file is rejected; refill reminders for repeat buyers. Flip the funnel above to “After treatment” to see the scenario.

    Assumes the checkout-to-order rate holds at 84.7%. It is a what-if to size the prize and prioritise, to be validated with an A/B test.

    Case 03KYC onboardingAnonymised · as-is → to-be

    Three hand-offs to say yes.

    01 Presenting problemWhat users and operations felt

    Customers needed KYC before activation, but the process was manual: documents uploaded, then re-keyed, then checked by hand.

    An anonymised illustration of the requirements work I did at imcure. The one measured result is real and on my resume: manual verification time cut by 30%. Every other figure on this case is a labelled assumption.

    02 DiagnosisAs-is map: where time and rework sit

    Mapping the as-is flow exposes three hand-offs and one rework loop: every invalid document sends the customer back to the start, and an operator re-types data the customer already entered.

    03 TreatmentTo-be map, BRD excerpt, user stories

    BRD excerpt

    BR-3 The system shall extract identity fields from uploaded documents and pre-fill the form.
    BR-5 Only mismatches shall be routed to a manual-review queue, each with a reason code.
    BR-7 All verification outcomes shall be audit-logged.

    User stories and acceptance criteria

    #StoryAcceptance criteria
    1As a customer, I upload my ID once so I don’t retype details.Given a clear ID image, fields pre-fill in under 5 seconds and stay editable.
    2As a customer, I see why a document was rejected.Rejection shows a reason and a retry; no entered data is lost.
    3As an ops reviewer, I only see exceptions.Queue holds only mismatches, each with a reason code.
    4As compliance, I need an audit trail.Every decision stores who, what and when; exportable.
    5As a product owner, I track drop-off by step.Dashboard shows completion rate per step, daily.

1. Clinic operations dashboard

Synthetic data (seeded generator, 12 weeks, one multi-therapist physiotherapy clinic).

Problem

A clinic owner saw full diaries but flat revenue. No single view of appointments, no-shows, therapist utilisation and revenue per week.

Approach

Defined four KPIs (booked appointments, no-show %, therapist utilisation %, realised revenue), modelled appointment/therapist/invoice tables, and built a weekly dashboard (Power BI-style, rendered here with Chart.js).

SQL used

SELECT DATE_TRUNC('week', a.slot_start) AS wk,
       COUNT(*)                                            AS booked,
       100.0*SUM(CASE WHEN a.status='no_show' THEN 1 END)/COUNT(*) AS no_show_pct,
       SUM(a.minutes_delivered)*100.0/SUM(t.available_minutes)     AS utilisation_pct,
       SUM(i.amount)                                       AS revenue
FROM appointments a
JOIN therapists t ON t.id=a.therapist_id
LEFT JOIN invoices i ON i.appointment_id=a.id
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

Insight

In this synthetic scenario no-shows concentrate in first-visit slots booked more than 5 days ahead, and utilisation dips on Tuesday/Friday afternoons.

Business impact (illustrative)

A reminder plus overbooking rule for far-ahead first visits takes no-shows from ~15% to ~11% and lifts utilisation from ~61% to ~74% over the 12 weeks; average weekly revenue in the last 3 weeks is about 24% above the first 3 in this synthetic model.

2. E-pharmacy funnel analysis

Synthetic numbers. Structure inspired by funnel tracking work on a real e-pharmacy / healthcare platform (IMCURE); no actual company data is shown.

Problem

Traffic was growing but orders were not. Where do visitors drop, and do first-time buyers come back?

Approach

Instrumented events (visit, product view, add-to-cart, checkout, order), built the funnel and monthly cohort retention in SQL, then ranked fixes by recoverable orders.

CohortM0M1M2M3
Jan100%24%17%14%
Feb100%27%19%15%
Mar100%31%22%–

SQL used

WITH first_order AS (
  SELECT user_id, DATE_TRUNC('month', MIN(ordered_at)) AS cohort FROM orders GROUP BY 1)
SELECT f.cohort,
       DATE_PART('month', AGE(DATE_TRUNC('month', o.ordered_at), f.cohort)) AS m,
       COUNT(DISTINCT o.user_id) * 1.0 / COUNT(DISTINCT f.user_id) AS retention
FROM first_order f JOIN orders o USING (user_id)
GROUP BY 1,2 ORDER BY 1,2;

Insight

The largest leak is cart → checkout (shipping and prescription-upload friction), not traffic. Repeat rate is highest for chronic-medicine refill buyers.

Business impact (illustrative)

Recommendations: guest checkout, upload-prescription at cart, refill reminders. Lifting cart→checkout from 38% to 48% adds ~26% more orders on the same traffic in this synthetic model.

3. KYC onboarding: as-is → to-be redesign

Anonymised illustration of the type of requirements work done at IMCURE. No invented company metrics; KPI targets are labelled assumptions.

Problem

Customer onboarding needed KYC but users abandoned the flow and operations re-keyed documents by hand.

Approach

Mapped the as-is process, found hand-offs and rework, designed a to-be flow, then wrote the BRD excerpt and user stories.

BRD excerpt

BR-3. The system shall extract identity fields from uploaded documents and pre-fill the form. BR-5. Only mismatches shall be routed to a manual-review queue with a reason code. BR-7. All verification outcomes shall be audit-logged.

User stories (acceptance criteria)

#StoryAcceptance criteria
1As a customer I upload my ID once so I don't retype details.Given a clear ID image, fields pre-fill in under 5s; user can edit.
2As a customer I see why a document was rejected.Rejection shows reason + retry; no data lost.
3As an ops reviewer I only see exceptions.Queue contains only mismatches; each has a reason code.
4As compliance I need an audit trail.Every decision stores who/what/when; exportable.
5As a product owner I track drop-off by step.Dashboard shows completion rate per step, daily.

Tools

Excel (process timing), SQL (drop-off by step), BPMN-style mapping, BRD and user-story writing.

The only real, resume-verified outcome here: the redesign cut manual verification time by 30% at IMCURE. The table below is a synthetic target model.

Finding the leak in an e-pharmacy funnel

Synthetic data. Every number in this case study is synthetic, generated for illustration with a deterministic script. No real company, customer or order data is shown.

Key findings

  • Of 1,900 carts, 1,180 never reached checkout (62%): the steepest fall after the product page, and the step where shipping cost and prescription upload first appear.
  • Retention is improving cohort over cohort (month-1 return 24%, 27%, 31%), so the problem is conversion before checkout, not loyalty after it.
  • Scenario: lifting cart-to-checkout from 38% to 48% would add about 27% more orders on the same traffic, if the checkout-to-order rate holds.

Context

An online pharmacy saw visits climb while orders stayed flat. Two questions followed: where do visitors leave, and do first-time buyers come back? The structure reflects the funnel and conversion tracking I set up at imcure (Google Ads conversion tracking, partner-API journeys); the data here is entirely synthetic and the SQL runs on the synthetic dataset.

Approach

I modelled the journey as a stream of events (visit, product view, add to cart, checkout, order), built the funnel and monthly retention cohorts in SQL, and ranked candidate fixes by the number of orders each could recover.

Analysis

1. Funnel

Browsing drop-off is expected. Among shoppers with intent, 1,180 of 1,900 carts (62%) never reach checkout (Fig. 1). Cart to checkout converts at 37.9%, while checkout to order converts at 84.7%.

2. Retention by first-order cohort

Month-1 return improves from 24% to 27% to 31% across cohorts, so loyalty after the first order is not the constraint. Share of each cohort that ordered again (synthetic):

First-order cohortMonth 0Month 1Month 2Month 3
2026-01100%24%17%14%
2026-02100%27%19%15%
2026-03100%31%22%-
WITH first_order AS (
  SELECT user_id, MIN(substr(ordered_at, 1, 7)) AS cohort
  FROM   orders GROUP BY user_id)
SELECT f.cohort,
       (CAST(substr(o.ordered_at, 1, 4) AS INT) - CAST(substr(f.cohort, 1, 4) AS INT)) * 12
       + CAST(substr(o.ordered_at, 6, 2) AS INT) - CAST(substr(f.cohort, 6, 2) AS INT) AS month_n,
       ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(DISTINCT o.user_id)
           / (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM first_order x WHERE x.cohort = f.cohort), 0)       AS retention_pct
FROM   first_order f
JOIN   orders o ON o.user_id = f.user_id
GROUP  BY 1, 2
ORDER  BY 1, 2;

Recommendations

Three changes, ranked by orders recoverable.

RecommendationWhat changesHow to test
1. Guest checkoutNo forced sign-up before payment.A/B test on the checkout page.
2. Prescription upload at the cartUpload earlier, with a clear reason if a file is rejected.A/B test; track rejected-upload rate.
3. Refill remindersFor repeat buyers, ahead of the refill date.Holdout group; track month-1 return.

Impact

Scenario, not a measurement. If cart-to-checkout rose from 37.9% to 48%, checkouts would rise from 720 to 912, and with the checkout-to-order rate unchanged at 84.7%, orders from 610 to about 773 (+26.7%) on the same 10,000 visits. This sizes the prize and prioritises the work; it needs validating with an A/B test.
